# Phi Voice Tells — prose patterns that must not appear

Binds on every document written for Phi to read or as Phi (artifacts, papers, briefings, reports). Full voice model: `gestalt/knowledge/the-relevant-entry.md` (`^tmlr-corpus`, `^documents-for-phi`, `^formal-ai-tells`).

**The meta-rule: an authentic Phi move deployed by formula becomes an AI tell.** His signature moves (title-comma-and headings, verdict fragments, appositive glosses) are earned by their surrounding argument and used sparsely; transplanting them densely into ordinary explanatory prose produces pastiche he rejects on sight (flagged 2026-08-30 on the rewrite-kit artifact, verbatim: "this prose is extremely ai").

Never write, in any register:

- **Enumerative meta-framing openers**: "Three properties make it the right table.", "Each stage has delivered a result." Announce-then-list scaffolding; state the content directly instead.
- **Fragment parallel pairs and snap-backs**: "No registration, no license restriction."; rhetorical-question openers with a parallel answer.
- **Trailing verdict sentences** (banned outright by Phi 2026-10-07): a paragraph that ends "evidence. evidence. Short verdict." such as "The first map was incomplete and the second was outdated. Neither reported the fact." Fold the label into the evidence sentence or end on the evidence.
- **Trailing emphatic adverbs**: "…the returned value is the answer, exactly." The punch word adds drama, not information.
- **Multi-gloss parentheticals**: "(SODA, the Socrata Open Data API, is the interface NYC Open Data exposes; its query language, SoQL, reads like SQL)". One gloss per parenthetical; a second gloss gets its own sentence.
- **Borrowed title formulas on mundane headings**: "The data source, and why it is the right one". Reference-doc headings are plain noun phrases; the title-comma-and pattern belongs to paper section titles where the section argues the claim.
- **Rhetorical questions, Moreover/Additionally**: zero, always (the-relevant-entry hard rules). **Furthermore** is allowed once per formal document as a second-point connective, because Phi's own AAAI-27 DC abstract uses it (designated the formal exemplar 2026-10-12, `the-relevant-entry.md` ^formal-exemplars-2026-10-12). In that register, enumerations run as one long serial sentence and parallel asks or cited clauses may be joined by semicolons; the Hemingway rule governs chat, notes and documents written for him, not his formal prose. **Em dashes: allowed since 2026-09-03.** Phi is deliberately adopting them ("em dashes are okay ... im trying to use them more, just make sure they are accurate"). The check is accuracy, not count: a pair sets off a true parenthetical and the sentence must still parse with the pair removed; a single dash marks a genuine break or summary, not a comma substitute; spacing stays consistent within a document (Phi currently writes spaced " — "). Never add em dashes to his prose on my own initiative; never strip the ones he wrote.

Default to plain informative sentences. At most one signature move per section, where the content genuinely earns it. Before delivering: check every em dash parses (pair removable, single one marks a real break), check no symbol precedes its introduction, count examples per concept (≥1), and scan for the five patterns above.
